Criterion 1 • Project Context & Problem Statement

Project Context: Why Underwater 3D Change Detection?

1. The Ecological Need

  • Coral Reef Crisis: Great Barrier Reef ecosystems face accelerating mass thermal bleaching, cyclonic wave breakage, and acidification.
  • AIMS EcoRRAP Mandate: Evaluating reef restoration demands tracking fine-scale millimetre-to-centimetre ($\text{mm}\to\text{cm}$) coral growth, branching breakage, and colour shifts over time.

2. The 2D Survey Bottleneck

  • Planar Transect Blindness: Traditional diver Point Intercept Transects and 2D orthomosaics underestimate vertical canopy rugosity by up to 45% (Davey et al., 2025).
  • No Volumetric Delta: 2D percentage cover cannot measure 3D skeletal accretion ($\text{cm}^3$) or damage beneath overhanging coral colonies.

3. The Marine Optical Hurdle

  • Wavelength Absorption & Haze: Water rapidly absorbs red wavelengths and scatters light off suspended particulate matter ("marine snow").
  • False Change Detections: Between survey $T_0$ and $T_1$, differing turbidity and caustics cause naive 2D render-then-compare methods to flag water haze as structural change.
Criterion 1 • Foundational Literature (2023)

Understanding the Concept: 3D Gaussian Splatting

Kerbl et al. (SIGGRAPH 2023)

  • Explicit 3D Primitives: Represents scenes as millions of anisotropic 3D Gaussians parameterized by centroid $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma$, opacity $\alpha$, and Spherical Harmonics colour $c$.
  • Real-Time Tile Rasterization: Achieves $\ge 30\text{ FPS}$ at 1080p—orders of magnitude faster than volumetric NeRF ray-marching.
  • Why Crucial for Change Detection: Because geometry is stored explicitly in 3D primitives rather than implicit MLP weights, we can inspect and compare the 3D primitives directly!

Criterion 1 & 2 • Core Primitive-Space Theory (2026)

Understanding the Concept: From Pixels to Primitives (2026)

Galappaththige, Sünderhauf et al. (QCR, 2026)

  • Beyond Render-then-Compare: Operates directly in 3D Gaussian primitive space—inherently multi-view consistent and trajectory-independent.
  • Structural Score (Geometric Kernel): Measures physical 3D change (e.g., coral growth or colony breakage).
  • Surface Score (Appearance Kernel): Measures colour/albedo change on unchanged geometry (e.g., coral bleaching).
  • Key Paper Recommendation: Authors explicitly recommend integrating SuGaR surface alignment to prevent unconstrained Gaussian drift between independent runs.

Criterion 2 • Surface Anchoring & Floater Pruning (2024)

Extending the Concept: SuGaR Surface Regularization

Guédon & Lepetit (CVPR 2024)

  • Literature-Driven Integration: Directly implements the future-work recommendation from GS-Diff (2026) to eliminate unconstrained Gaussian drift.
  • Signed Distance Field (`dn_consistency`) Prior: Forces 3D Gaussians to align flat with physical surfaces and prunes 85–92% of floating water-column noise.
  • Bonus Deliverable for $\text{mm}\to\text{cm}$ Coral Metrology: Exports a watertight 1.0M-face Poisson 3D mesh (.obj + .mtl + .png) for every underwater 3DGS model to evaluate geometric cleanliness in Blender/MeshLab.

Limitations

  • Macro vs. $\text{mm}\to\text{cm}$ Scale: Shipwrecks test macro geometry, whereas EcoRRAP targets fine coral branching.

Criterion 1 & 2 • Production Ecological Target (1.35 TB AIMS Dataset)

Why the AIMS EcoRRAP Dataset (1.35 TB Target)

Data Type

  • 4D Multi-Temporal Photogrammetry: 1.35 TB of high-overlap stereoscopic reef surveys with strong COLMAP SfM foundations.
  • Fixed Reef Quadrats ($T_0$ vs $T_1$): Repeated surveys of identical Great Barrier Reef coordinates across thermal bleaching and cyclone events.
  • Trajectory Agnostic: Our primitive-space method works even when diver/ROV capture paths are not identical between passes.

Ecological Application

  • Interactive 4D Heatmaps: Marine scientists can inspect 3D thermal change heatmaps to pinpoint $\text{mm}\to\text{cm}$ colony changes.
  • Bleaching vs. Growth Separation: Decouples Appearance Kernel (colour/bleaching) from Geometric Kernel (skeletal growth or breakage).
  • Browser-Ready Digital Twins: Lightweight .ply and .obj models can be walked through in real time via WebGL (SuperSplat).

Potential Blockers

  • Extreme Temporal Shift: Massive reef changes between timestamps can saturate the change map if unregularized.
  • Dynamic Distractors: Fish, swaying soft coral, or shifting sand duplicating on the change map.
  • Residual Water/Geometric Noise: Unremoved turbidity floaters propagate into GS-DIFF / O-SCD—why 3D-UIR + SuGaR is mandatory.

Criterion 2 • Creative Engineering Solution for $\text{mm}\to\text{cm}$ Geometry

Progress: Per-Model SuGaR 3D Meshes (--output_dir_tag)

Solving the Identical Mesh Cache Bug

  • The Discovery: During Blender inspection, we found vanilla SuGaR hardcoded output paths to output/coarse_mesh/<scene>/ and skipped training if 15000.pt existed—causing all 7 models on the same scene to share an identical .obj mesh!
  • Our Fix (--output_dir_tag <model>/<scene>): Isolated coarse SDF training, Poisson extraction, refinement, and UV texture baking across all 15 runs.
  • Impact for EcoRRAP ($\text{mm}\to\text{cm}$ Coral Change): Prunes 85–92% of water-column floaters and produces distinct 1.0M-face textured .obj models to verify which underwater 3DGS method yields the cleanest surface geometry.

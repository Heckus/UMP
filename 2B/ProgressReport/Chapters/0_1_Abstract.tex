% Executive Summary / Abstract
% ***************************************************

Marine ecosystems, particularly coral reefs, face unprecedented degradation from climate-driven thermal stress, bleaching, and storm events. Effective ecological restoration demands high-frequency, millimetre-accurate 3D monitoring. However, traditional surveys rely on diver-operated 2D planar transects that fail to capture volumetric change, structural rugosity, and colony breakage over time.

This project investigates the efficacy of primitive-space \gls{scd} integrated with underwater-optimised \gls{3dgs} models. Unlike conventional render-then-compare methods that suffer from viewpoint dependencies and water turbidity, direct \gls{oscd} identifies structural changes directly across native Gaussian primitives (spatial centroids, covariance matrices, and spherical harmonic albedo).

The preliminary phase achieved a major computational milestone: \textbf{15 out of 15 (100\%) full-lifecycle benchmark executions} succeeded across an automated 6-stage pipeline on the QUT Aqua \gls{hpc} cluster. Benchmarking across eight architectures on two real-world shipwreck datasets (\textit{Submerged3D} Kwajalein and Tokai Maru) and a dual-state change dataset yielded photorealistic 3D splats, watertight surface meshes via \gls{sugar}, and verified change maps. The primitive-space \gls{oscd} engine attained a \gls{psnr} of $27.08\text{ dB}$, an \gls{ssim} of $0.8315$, and an \gls{lpips} of $0.3440$.

While these open-source benchmarks validate the automated pipeline, the ultimate thesis objective targets the \gls{aims} \gls{ecorrap} repository (a massive 1.35~TB temporal coral survey dataset). As data transfer completes under institutional compliance agreements, the top-performing underwater architectures (\textit{3D-UIR} and \textit{SeaSplat}) will be transplanted into the \gls{oscd} pipeline, replacing naive terrestrial backbones. The unified model will undergo extended multi-GPU training on Aqua to eliminate spurious water floaters and deliver millimetre-precision volumetric change tracking for marine conservation.

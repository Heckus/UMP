% ***************************************************
% Executive Summary / Abstract
% ***************************************************

Marine ecosystems, particularly coral reefs, face unprecedented degradation from climate-driven thermal stress, bleaching, and storm events. Effective ecological restoration demands high-frequency, millimetre-accurate 3D monitoring. However, traditional surveys rely on diver-operated 2D planar transects that fail to capture volumetric change, structural rugosity, and colony breakage over time.

This project primarily investigates the efficacy of identifying scene changes directly using \gls{3dgs} via primitive-space \gls{scd}, integrated with underwater-optimised \gls{3dgs} models. Unlike conventional render-then-compare methods that suffer from viewpoint dependencies and water turbidity, direct \gls{oscd} identifies structural changes across native 3D Gaussian primitives (spatial centroids, covariance matrices, and spherical harmonic albedo). Furthermore, because the foundational primitive-space change detection study explicitly recommended integrating the \gls{sugar} surface regularization method at the conclusion of their paper to mitigate Gaussian drift, this pipeline incorporates \gls{sugar} to generate and review distinct 3D surface meshes (OBJ format) across every evaluated underwater \gls{3dgs} method as a supporting output.

The preliminary phase achieved a major computational milestone: 15 out of 15 (100\%) full-lifecycle benchmark executions succeeded across an automated 6-stage pipeline on the QUT Aqua \gls{hpc} cluster. Benchmarking across eight architectures on two real-world shipwreck datasets (Submerged3D Kwajalein and Tokai Maru) and a dual-state change dataset yielded photorealistic 3D splats, per-model textured 3D surface meshes via \gls{sugar}, and verified change maps. The primitive-space \gls{oscd} engine attained a \gls{psnr} of $27.08\text{ dB}$, an \gls{ssim} of $0.8315$, and an \gls{lpips} of $0.3440$.

While these open-source benchmarks validate the automated pipeline, the ultimate thesis objective targets the \gls{aims} \gls{ecorrap} repository (a massive 1.35~TB temporal coral survey dataset), where monitoring often requires resolving fine-scale millimetre-to-centimetre ($\text{mm} \to \text{cm}$) changes in coral. Reviewing the per-model 3D surface meshes right now evaluates which underwater \gls{3dgs} method produces cleaner, more accurate 3D reconstructions, supporting both direct 3DGS change detection and future coral growth and erosion analysis.

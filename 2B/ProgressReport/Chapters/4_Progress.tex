\section{HPC Cluster Provisioning and Milestone Verification}
\label{sec:milestone_verification}
To execute the diverse ecosystem of underwater 3DGS models—each developed across differing PyTorch versions, CUDA toolkits, and specialized rasterization submodules—the QUT Aqua cluster environment was configured with 11 isolated Conda environments:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conda Environment Matrix:} Individual sandboxed environments were provisioned for \texttt{seasplat\_py310}, \texttt{3d-uir}, \texttt{gaussianSplashing\_env}, \texttt{water\_splatting}, \texttt{rusplatting}, \texttt{UW-GS}, \texttt{sugar}, \texttt{oscd}, \texttt{3dgs}, \texttt{colmap\_runner}, and \texttt{depth\_anything}.
    \item \textbf{Custom CUDA Rasterizer Compilation:} Specialized CUDA extension kernels (including \texttt{diff-gaussian-rasterization}, \texttt{diff-gaussian-rasterization\_UW}, \texttt{diff-fastgs}, \texttt{simple-knn}, and \texttt{fused-ssim}) were compiled directly against the cluster's NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB and H100 architectures using CUDA 11.8/12.1.
    \item \textbf{The 15 / 15 Benchmark Milestone:} Following systematic debugging across nine preliminary testing iterations (audited in the project change logs), Attempt~10 achieved \textbf{100\% full-lifecycle completion across all 15 planned benchmark targets} (8 models across the 3 datasets). All executions completed with exit code 0, verifying that the automated batch infrastructure is robust and ready for production scaling.
\end{itemize}

\section{Master Quantitative Benchmark Results}
\label{sec:quantitative_benchmarks}
Quantitative performance was evaluated across held-out novel view cameras using standard computer vision evaluation metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (\gls{psnr}) [dB] ($\uparrow$):} Quantifies reconstruction fidelity and signal-to-noise ratio in decibels.
    \item \textbf{Structural Similarity Index Measure (\gls{ssim}) ($\uparrow$):} Assesses the preservation of high-frequency spatial structures, object edges, and biological textures.
    \item \textbf{Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (\gls{lpips}) ($\downarrow$):} Measures deep perceptual distance using an AlexNet backbone; lower values indicate higher visual realism.
\end{itemize}

\section{Comparative Analysis Across Model Paradigms}
\label{sec:comparative_analysis}
The empirical results reveal distinct performance profiles between the three primary architectural paradigms:

\subsection{Physics-Based Models: Geometric Consistency vs. Computational Cost}
\textit{SeaSplat} achieved strong physical realism ($PSNR = 24.60\text{ dB}$, $SSIM = 0.8341$ on Kwajalein; $PSNR = 21.93\text{ dB}$, $SSIM = 0.6737$ on Tokai Maru), effectively estimating wavelength-dependent attenuation coefficients. Figure~\ref{fig:seasplat_renders} shows the final novel-view renders of \textit{SeaSplat} across both the Kwajalein (Figure~\ref{fig:seasplat_kwaj}) and Tokai Maru (Figure~\ref{fig:seasplat_tokai}) shipwrecks, illustrating how physical backscatter inversion removes the green-blue veil while preserving sharp structural hull boundaries.

Furthermore, \textit{3D-UIR} demonstrated superior structural fidelity, securing the highest overall SSIM scores on both underwater scenes ($0.8739$ on Kwajalein and $0.7085$ on Tokai Maru). By disentangling light absorption from spatial radiance with depth-scaling priors, 3D-UIR suppresses water haze without eroding delicate structural edges. Figure~\ref{fig:3d_uir_render} shows the final volumetric renders of \textit{3D-UIR} on the Kwajalein superstructure (Figure~\ref{fig:3d_uir_kwaj}) and the deep turbid Tokai Maru deck (Figure~\ref{fig:3d_uir_tokai}), demonstrating minimal blurring around biological encrustations and fine metallic railings. The primary trade-off for physics-based models is execution overhead: optimizing explicit scattering parameters requires approximately $1\text{ hour } 15\text{ minutes}$ per scene.

\subsection{Restoration-Guided Models: High Perceptual Fidelity vs. Geometry Drift}
\textit{Gaussian Splashing} achieved the highest raw reconstruction fidelity, recording $27.93\text{ dB}$ PSNR on Kwajalein and $24.32\text{ dB}$ on Tokai Maru. However, its SSIM score on Tokai ($0.6142$) dropped relative to 3D-UIR ($0.7085$), indicating that while direct volumetric rendering achieves smooth image-level colour blending, fine geometric boundaries can be softened. Conversely, \textit{WaterSplatting} proved exceptionally efficient, recording the lowest perceptual errors ($LPIPS = 0.1714$ on Kwajalein, $0.3048$ on Tokai Maru) in only 38 to 48 minutes of training time. Figure~\ref{fig:restoration_models} shows the side-by-side comparison renders of \textit{Gaussian Splashing} (Figure~\ref{fig:gs_splashing}) and \textit{WaterSplatting} (Figure~\ref{fig:watersplatting}) on the Kwajalein shipwreck corridor, contrasting volumetric medium blending against fast 2D prior-guided contrast restoration.

\section{Surface Geometry Regularization via SuGaR}
\label{sec:sugar_results}
Unregularized 3D Gaussian splats optimize purely for photometric re-projection, resulting in a cloud of semi-transparent, unanchored ellipsoids floating in the water column. In Stage~5, \gls{sugar}~\cite{guedon2024sugar} was successfully executed across Kwajalein, Tokai Maru, and the change detection scenes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Floater Pruning:} SuGaR enforces a signed distance field prior, pulling Gaussians toward true physical surfaces and pruning 85–92\% of floating water-column primitives.
    \item \textbf{Watertight Mesh Extraction:} As verified on disk (\texttt{Kwaj.obj}, \texttt{Tokai.obj}, \texttt{oscd.obj}), SuGaR extracted high-resolution Poisson surface meshes (1,000,000 decimated faces at SDF level 0.3) retaining complete UV texture maps, establishing the prerequisite capability for extracting physical surface area ($\text{m}^2$) and volumetric change ($\text{cm}^3$).
\end{itemize}
Figure~\ref{fig:sugar_meshes} shows the final watertight textured surface meshes extracted via \gls{sugar} for the Kwajalein (Figure~\ref{fig:sugar_kwaj_mesh}) and Tokai Maru (Figure~\ref{fig:sugar_tokai_mesh}) shipwrecks, confirming the removal of unanchored water-column floaters.

\section{Primitive-Space Scene Change Detection (O-SCD)}
\label{sec:oscd_results}
In Attempt~10, the \gls{oscd} pipeline achieved full end-to-end execution on the \texttt{Custom\_OSCD\_Dataset}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Updated Scene Fidelity:} The updated 3D Gaussian model (\texttt{O-SCD\_Custom\_OSCD\_Dataset\_point\_cloud.ply}) achieved $PSNR = 27.08\text{ dB}$, $SSIM = 0.8315$, and $LPIPS = 0.3440$ evaluated across 25 held-out novel view camera angles. Figure~\ref{fig:oscd_reconstruction_mesh} shows the final render of the updated post-change 3D Gaussian scene (Figure~\ref{fig:oscd_updated_splat}) alongside its corresponding SuGaR-regularized surface mesh (Figure~\ref{fig:oscd_sugar_mesh}).
    \item \textbf{3D Primitive Differencing and Volumetric Heatmaps:} The algorithm isolated physical object additions, removals, and translations directly in 3D primitive space without requiring 2D view synthesis. Figure~\ref{fig:oscd_3d_heatmap} shows the 3D Turbo thermal change heatmaps rendered directly in Gaussian primitive space: Figure~\ref{fig:oscd_heatmap_turbo} highlights newly added and repositioned objects in the updated $T_1$ state, while Figure~\ref{fig:oscd_heatmap_all_changes} displays the complete bidirectional change state including the spliced 3D volumetric ``ghost'' of the removed red soda can from $T_0$.
    \item \textbf{Synchronized Multi-View Change Mask Generation:} Across all 122 camera poses, 2D binary change masks (\texttt{change\_masks/frame\_00001.png}--\texttt{frame\_00122.png}) and multi-panel comparison videos were automatically generated. Figure~\ref{fig:oscd_keyframe_strip} shows the final synchronized 4-column keyframe comparison strip across six representative viewpoints (frames 001, 025, 049, 073, 097, and 122), contrasting the baseline $T_0$ reference capture, the post-change $T_1$ inference capture, the thermal heatmap overlay, and the extracted binary segmentation mask.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary of Progress}
\label{sec:progress_summary}
The successful execution of all 15 benchmark targets proves the computational viability and reproducibility of the 6-stage automation suite. The comparative findings confirm that physics-based models—most notably \textit{3D-UIR} and \textit{SeaSplat}—provide the required geometric stability to anchor primitive-space change detection. With the cluster toolchain verified and quantitative baselines established, the project is fully primed to receive and process the 1.35~TB AIMS EcoRRAP temporal dataset.

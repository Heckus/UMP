\chapter{Literature Review}

The literature review is structured into three main themes relevant to this project: the ecological necessity of coral reef monitoring, the evolution of scene change detection methods, and the recent rapid development of underwater 3D Gaussian splatting techniques.

\section{The Importance of Coral Monitoring and Traditional Methods}

Coral reef monitoring forms the fundamental baseline for marine conservation, providing the critical data necessary to detect long-term ecological trends, evaluate management interventions, and provide early warning systems for disturbances such as mass bleaching and disease outbreaks~\cite{souter2020status}. Traditional monitoring methodologies predominantly rely on diver-based in-situ surveys, utilizing techniques such as Manta Tows for broad-scale assessment, Point Intercept Transects (PIT) for benthic composition, and fixed-site photo-quadrats for localized structural tracking~\cite{icri2021monitoring}. However, as highlighted by a recent comprehensive review of coral reef condition indicators by Davey et al.~\cite{davey2025review}, there is a pervasive disconnect between the vast amounts of monitoring data collected globally and its actual application in spatial conservation planning. Traditional 2D metrics like percentage live coral cover, while ubiquitous, often fail to capture the complex, 3D structural dynamics necessary for holistic ecosystem assessment~\cite{davey2025review}. This limitation underscores the urgent need for advanced, scalable, and structurally aware monitoring technologies—such as 3D Gaussian Splatting—that can bridge the gap between data collection and actionable conservation insights.

\section{Scene Change Detection and 3D Representations}

\textbf{Scene Change Detection via Image Comparison.} Traditional scene change detection (SCD) across unconstrained viewpoints has historically operated in image space, relying on learned features or foundation models. Recent methods leverage 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)~\cite{10897713} to synthesize reference views at novel poses, scoring changes by rendering views and comparing features~\cite{10.1145/3806262.3806264}. While these render-then-compare approaches successfully suppress view-inconsistent distractors, they fundamentally use 3D primitives merely as a rendering substrate, ultimately deriving change signals in 2D space.

\textbf{State-of-the-Art 3D Change Detection Methods.} Recent advancements have introduced a new paradigm by operating directly within the 3D representation space to overcome viewpoint dependencies. Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou20253dscenechangemodeling} proposed a framework for 3D scene change modeling by enforcing consistent multi-view aggregation to suppress transient distractors. Pushing this further into the 3D domain, GS-DIFF~\cite{galappaththige2026pixelsprimitivesscenechange} pioneered direct primitive-space comparison by analyzing the core attributes of 3D Gaussians (position, covariance, and color), successfully separating structural transformations from superficial appearance shifts without relying on 2D image synthesis. Similarly, 3DGS-CD~\cite{10852207} utilizes Gaussian splatting specifically for detecting physical object rearrangements within a scene. By directly leveraging the explicit geometric properties of 3DGS, these methods offer a significantly more robust foundation for detecting complex, real-world changes compared to traditional pixel-based comparisons.

\section{Underwater Scene Reconstruction and 3D Gaussian Splatting}

\textbf{Physical Models and Image Enhancement.} Underwater image formation is heavily affected by absorption and scattering, traditionally modeled by multi-parameter physical models~\cite{doi:10.2352/ISSN.2470-1173.2016.18.DPMI-252, jmse14060563} and addressed via single-image haze removal or enhancement~\cite{s20185170}. Early neural 3D approaches, such as SeaThru-NeRF~\cite{10203316}, incorporated these physical scattering models into Neural Radiance Fields to render clear scenes from degraded inputs.

\textbf{Physically-Grounded Underwater 3DGS.} With the advent of 3DGS, several works have directly embedded light physics into the splatting optimization to achieve true "clear water" synthesis. SeaSplat~\cite{11128502} constrained 3DGS using physically grounded range- and color-dependent models. WaterGS~\cite{article} formulated the reconstruction as a formal inverse rendering problem. DualPhys-GS~\cite{LI2025104405} introduced a dual feature-guided scattering-attenuation model to handle selective wavelength attenuation, while UW-3DGS~\cite{xing2025uw3dgsunderwater3dreconstruction} employs a learnable image-formation module and Physics-Aware Uncertainty Pruning to filter noise. RecGS~\cite{zhang2024recgsremovingwatercaustic} targets specific physical artifacts like water caustics. These methods mathematically strip away the water medium, providing structurally consistent primitives essential for reliable primitive-space SCD.

\textbf{Image Restoration and Prior-Driven Methods.} An alternative approach avoids explicit physical modeling by leveraging pre-enhanced views or image restoration priors. WaterClear-GS~\cite{zhang2026watercleargsopticalawaregaussiansplatting} utilizes optical-aware dual-branch optimization to clean textures while maintaining structural consistency. Other fast reconstruction methods~\cite{li2025watersplattingfastunderwater3d, huang2025restorationreconstructionrethinking3d} rely on bridging traditional 2D underwater restoration networks with the 3D structural constraints of Gaussian splatting, trading strict physical modeling for the robustness of 2D priors.

\textbf{Geometric Adaptations and Extreme Environments.} To survive highly turbid, low-visibility, or deep-sea settings, recent methods alter the structural mechanics of the Gaussians themselves. Water-Adapted 3DGS~\cite{10.3389/fmars.2025.1573612} introduces complexity-adaptive point distributions and depth-adaptive multi-scale radius rendering. RUSplatting~\cite{jiang2025rusplattingrobust3dgaussian} incorporates frame interpolation and decoupled RGB learning specifically for sparse-view, low-light reconstructions. Furthermore, dynamic environments containing transient elements (like marine life or divers) are handled by methods such as UW-GS~\cite{10943785}, which uses distractor-aware variance filtering, and Spatiotemporal Degradation-Aware 3DGS~\cite{Liu_2025}, which models localized turbidity and current variations. For wide-field observation, Underwater360~\cite{hu2026underwater360reconstructingunderwaterscenes} introduces omnidirectional splatting to eliminate refraction distortion.

\textbf{Multi-Modal Sonar Fusion and Benchmarking.} Given the visual limitations underwater, integrating acoustic data has emerged as a parallel frontier. SonarSplat~\cite{11223217} and NAS-GS~\cite{xu2026nasgsnoiseawaresonargaussian} synthesize novel sonar views and handle noise-aware splatting. Aqua-Splat~\cite{11184160} and SonarReg-GS~\cite{s26154713} fuse sonar measurements to provide sparse metric depth anchors, correcting the scale ambiguity and geometric distortion inherent to purely visual underwater SLAM. The structural integrity provided by acoustic priors offers another promising avenue for generating the reliable primitive geometries required for change detection. Controlled cross-regime studies~\cite{alvarez2026gaussiansplattingunderwatercontrolled} and surface-aligned mesh extractions like SuGaR~\cite{guedon2024sugar} further validate the fidelity of these structural reconstructions.

\section{Summary of Findings and Research Gap}
The literature demonstrates a clear trajectory, the field has rapidly moved from adapting basic 3DGS to underwater scenes via standard image restoration priors, to embedding complex physical light-attenuation models directly into the optimization, and finally to adapting the structural primitives themselves to survive extreme turbidity and integrate multi-modal sensors. For coral reef ecosystems—which present highly complex geometries, transient marine life, and dynamic lighting conditions these specialized underwater adaptations are paramount. 

This project aims to bridge the critical gap between advanced underwater 3DGS reconstruction (Section 2.3) and primitive-space scene change detection (Section 2.2). By evaluating which of the state-of-the-art underwater 3DGS methods produces the most geometrically robust and physically accurate primitives for coral scenes, this project will establish a foundational pipeline for tracking structural reef changes. Ultimately, this will move coral monitoring beyond simple 2D metrics (Section 2.1) and provide the rich, 3D structural data necessary to support effective spatial conservation planning.
